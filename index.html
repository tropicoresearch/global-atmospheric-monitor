About the Dataset

NOAA CPC CORe provides global daily atmospheric analyses on a Gaussian grid. Anomalies are calculated relative to the 1991–2020 daily climatological annual cycle. The original grid is preserved during scientific processing. The globe displays a visualization texture derived from these values.

Scientific Interpretation

Positive temperature anomalies indicate warmer conditions than the climatological reference. Negative anomalies indicate cooler conditions.

Data & Methodology

How the latest available atmospheric analysis is transformed into the anomalies shown on the globe.

Data source & acknowledgement

The original data are from the Conventional Observation Reanalysis (CORe), produced by the NOAA/NCEP Climate Prediction Center (CPC) in collaboration with the Environmental Modeling Center (EMC) and the NOAA Physical Sciences Laboratory (PSL).

Daily ensemble-mean fields are obtained from the official NOAA Open Data Dissemination (NODD) cloud archive. We retain CORe's native 512 × 256 Gaussian grid during the scientific calculations.

NOAA CORe documentation ↗Official archive ↗

1991–2020 daily climatology

For each atmospheric field and each calendar day, a reference mean is computed from the 30 years spanning 1991–2020. The resulting annual cycle is smoothed with a 31-day centered moving window, wrapping across the end of December and the beginning of January.

The climatology is stored separately from the current analyses. For a leap-day analysis, the February 29 reference is interpolated as the average of the February 28 and March 1 climatologies.

Anomaly calculation

Each displayed anomaly is the difference between the daily-mean CORe analysis and the smoothed reference for the same calendar day. Precipitation-rate anomalies are converted to mm/day.

Wind-speed anomalies are calculated from the magnitude of the daily-mean horizontal wind vector and its corresponding reference climatology. Colors are scaled for visualization; adjusted limits do not change the underlying anomalies.

Latest common date & wind display

The monitor selects the most recent day for which all required FLX and PGB fields are available. All displayed anomalies therefore share one analysis date, shown above the globe. Products may lag the current day as NOAA updates its archive.

On wind-speed maps, optional stationary arrows show the direction of the original daily-mean U and V wind fields at 850 or 250 hPa. Arrow size is a display setting, not a quantitative measure of wind speed. The background colors continue to represent wind-speed anomalies.
